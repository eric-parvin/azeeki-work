About

Architecture, delivery, and the unglamorous work in between

Most of my recent work lives in Microsoft Fabric, Power BI, and Azure DevOps — designing governed data platforms, building semantic models people trust, and moving teams off legacy reporting without losing the business logic buried inside it. Before that I spent a decade in application performance, .NET, and DevOps engineering, which still shapes how I think about data platforms: they are production systems, and they fail in production ways.

I care about the part of this work that does not demo well. Whether a number in a report reconciles to the source system. Whether a refresh holds up at month-end. Whether the team inheriting the platform can actually operate it after I leave. Governance, validation, and knowledge transfer are not add-ons in my engagements — they are the engagement.

The other shift in how I work is AI, and I do not treat it as a feature. I write custom GitHub Copilot agent skills and prompt libraries that encode assessment and migration methodology into repeatable automation. Report inventory, dependency analysis, semantic model review, conversion estimation — work that used to take weeks of manual effort now takes days, with a human reviewing every output before it reaches a customer.

I am Microsoft certified as a Fabric Analytics Engineer Associate and in Azure AI Fundamentals, and I was accredited by Microsoft to scope and deliver its Power BI, Azure DevOps, and application modernization service offerings.
