A governance-first Fabric roadmap for a university foundation

Client University foundation Role Architect and advisor Scope Adoption planning and proof of concept

Problem

The foundation had decided to adopt Microsoft Fabric but had no plan for getting there. They had a mix of legacy paginated reports, aging ETL processes, a constituent management system they needed to coexist with, and a small team that would have to run whatever got built.

The risk was familiar: start building, discover governance problems late, and end up with an expensive platform that nobody trusts and nobody clearly owns. Capacity sizing, workspace structure, and access control decisions made casually in month one become very difficult to unwind in month twelve.

Adoption failures are rarely technical. They happen when sequencing, ownership, and governance decisions get deferred until after the platform is already in use.

Approach

I put governance first — not as a compliance exercise, but because those decisions constrain everything built afterward.

  • Ran discovery across current-state reporting, data sources, integration points, team capability, and the operating model they realistically could sustain.
  • Built a phased 30-60-90 roadmap with a prioritized backlog, explicit sequencing, dependencies, and named decision points rather than an undifferentiated task list.
  • Separated foundation work that had to happen first — tenant configuration, capacity strategy, workspace topology, identity and access — from workload delivery that could proceed in parallel once the foundation was set.
  • Identified a narrow, high-visibility proof of concept to prove platform value early and build internal support for the larger investment.

Solution

The roadmap covered platform foundation, workload delivery, and operating model, with status tracked against the backlog in recurring reviews.

  • Foundation. Tenant settings, capacity strategy and sizing, workspace design, identity and access controls, lifecycle management, and monitoring.
  • Workloads. Semantic model design, legacy paginated report migration assessment, ETL modernization from existing processes, and a coexistence strategy for the constituent system.
  • Operating model. CI/CD with source control and approval gates, cataloging and data protection controls, and a self-service model defining what business users could do independently versus what stayed centrally governed.

The proof of concept addressed a real gap: staff needed to record constituent interactions from mobile devices, and were using separate software to do it. I built a Power BI report with write-back to a Fabric SQL database using Python user-defined functions, letting staff submit data directly from a mobile-optimized report into governed storage.

Outcome

  • The proof of concept drove a production capacity purchase. The write-back demonstration became the primary reason the customer stood up their first paid Fabric capacity.
  • A sequenced plan replaced an open-ended wish list. Stakeholders could see what came first, what depended on what, and which decisions were still outstanding.
  • Governance decisions were made deliberately, before scale. Capacity, workspace structure, and access model were settled while they were still cheap to change.
  • A reusable framework. The planning structure and sequencing approach carried forward into later engagements.
