Unifying engineering data across a global audit organization

Client Global professional services firm Role Lead architect, end to end Scope 300-hour contracted engagement

Problem

A global audit organization ran its software delivery across dozens of separate Azure DevOps team projects. Each project had its own reporting, which meant every question at the portfolio level required someone to pull several reports and reconcile them by hand.

Leadership could not answer basic questions consistently. How much test coverage exists across the program? Where is quality slipping? Which teams are carrying the most unfinished work into the next cycle? The data existed — it was just fragmented across project boundaries that made sense operationally but not analytically.

The existing solution compounded the problem. A legacy SQL Server reporting database was fed by PowerShell extraction scripts that broke regularly and needed manual intervention, run by a team in a different time zone from the people waiting on the data. Reports took over seven hours to refresh end to end, against a stated need for data no more than fifteen minutes old.

The real problem was not refresh speed. It was that the organization had no single view of its own delivery and quality data. Refresh time was the symptom people complained about.

Approach

I started by separating the two problems: consolidation and performance. Fixing refresh on a fragmented model would have produced fast reports that still could not answer portfolio questions.

  • Inventoried entities across team projects to establish what work item and test data was genuinely common versus locally customized.
  • Designed a medallion architecture — raw ingestion, conformed and cleansed, then purpose-built serving layers aligned to how each audience actually consumed the data.
  • Modeled the serving layer around conformed dimensions so that work items, test results, iterations, and teams resolved consistently regardless of which project they originated in.
  • Replaced script-based extraction with governed pipelines, using watermark-based incremental processing so each run handled only what changed.
  • Sized and tuned capacity deliberately, treating compute consumption as a design constraint rather than something to discover after go-live.

Solution

The delivered platform was a Microsoft Fabric data warehouse consolidating work item and test data from across the entire DevOps organization into one enterprise reporting foundation.

  • Automated ingestion of work item and test entities across all team projects, removing the manual extraction dependency entirely.
  • Medallion-layer pipelines with incremental refresh supporting multiple loads per day.
  • Purpose-built warehouse models serving distinct reporting needs rather than one generic model attempting to satisfy every audience.
  • Power BI semantic models over the warehouse, so report authors worked against governed, conformed data instead of building their own extracts.
  • Capacity optimization that kept consumption within the provisioned tier while supporting the increased refresh cadence.

Outcome

  • A single enterprise view. Delivery, quality, and test coverage became visible across every team project without manual reconciliation — the outcome the engagement was actually about.
  • Refresh dropped from over 7 hours to 30 minutes. Reporting moved from overnight batch to near-real-time, meeting the fifteen-minute freshness requirement in practice.
  • A fragile manual process was eliminated. Scheduled pipeline runs replaced extraction scripts that required regular human intervention and caused downstream delays.
  • The customer expanded the platform. They planned to onboard ten additional team projects and increase their Fabric capacity — the clearest signal that the foundation held up.

The engagement ran multiple months from architecture through production readiness, delivered under a 300-hour contract.
