Teaching teams to run what I built

Clients Academic medical center, state government agency Role Curriculum designer and instructor Format Multi-day, instructor-led with labs

Problem

The fastest way to make a platform engagement fail is to leave behind something nobody internally knows how to operate. The build succeeds, the consultant departs, and within six months the platform is either frozen or quietly abandoned because the team cannot extend it.

Two engagements made this concrete. An academic medical center needed its analysts to build and manage their own Power BI content rather than queueing every request behind a small central team. A state government agency needed staff to understand DevOps reporting well enough to answer their own delivery questions.

Generic vendor training does not solve this. It teaches the product in the abstract, using sample data, disconnected from the environment and problems the audience actually faces. People leave able to follow a tutorial but not to do their job differently.

Adoption is not a training problem or a technology problem. It is a capability problem — and capability is built by having people do the work, in their context, with support.

Approach

  • Structured content in role-based tracks: report developers, self-service business users, and consumers have genuinely different needs and should not sit through each other's material.
  • Built hands-on labs as the backbone rather than an afterthought. Concepts were introduced, then immediately applied, because the applying is where learning happens.
  • Grounded examples in the customer's own scenarios instead of generic sample datasets.
  • Covered the full lifecycle — not just authoring, but publishing, sharing, refresh management, workspace roles, and what to do when something breaks.
  • Produced instructor guides, talking points, and participant materials so the customer could re-run portions internally afterward.
  • Provisioned and tested lab environments in advance, with support paths defined, so class time was not consumed by setup problems.

Solution

Multi-day instructor-led programs, delivered on site and remotely.

  • Power BI. Data shaping and transformation, semantic model design, report canvas and visual selection, calculations, publishing, workspace management, and refresh configuration — each module paired with a lab.
  • Azure DevOps reporting. Defining datasets from analytics views, shaping delivery data, building reports on work-tracking data, and managing report distribution.
  • Microsoft Fabric. Platform concepts, workspace and capacity structure, and how Fabric workloads relate to existing Power BI practice.
  • Supporting materials. Slide decks, lab guides, instructor notes, and participant communications covering access and prerequisites.

Outcome

  • Teams could operate independently. Participants left able to build, publish, and manage content without routing every request through a central bottleneck.
  • Materials outlasted the sessions. Instructor guides and labs let customers onboard new staff internally rather than re-purchasing training.
  • Training surfaced real problems. Working through labs in the customer's own environment exposed governance gaps, permission issues, and data-quality problems that would otherwise have appeared later in production.
  • Reusable delivery IP. The curriculum structure carried forward across engagements rather than being rebuilt each time.

Client identities withheld. Training approach described in general terms; no proprietary data, participant information, or confidential business detail is represented here. Course materials developed during prior employment would be rebuilt independently for commercial delivery.
