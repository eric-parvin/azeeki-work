## How the City's Contracting Process Works

1. **Approved contracting companies:** LADBS doesn't hire contractors directly — it works through roughly a half-dozen pre-approved staffing companies that meet the city's insurance and compliance requirements.
2. **Contract length:** Normally a 1-year contract. Right now is an unusual window — the city's master contract with these staffing companies expires **December 31**, so any contract signed now would only run through year-end, and everyone (not just Eric) would then need to be re-solicited/re-interviewed for a fresh ~1-year term once the master contract renews.
3. **Task Order Solicitation (TOS):** Shannon has already written the TOS for this specific position (Systems Architect Advanced / Azure Cloud specialist), covering scope, minimum qualifications (Microsoft certification — one of five acceptable certs), and required references (3). It's been handed to Hannah for formatting and is now sitting with the General Manager (John) for approval — the step it's currently stuck on.
4. **Once approved:** The TOS goes out to the approved staffing companies. Each one acknowledges by a set date, then submits a candidate resume plus a proposed hourly rate.
5. **Shortlisting & interview:** LADBS filters submitted candidates down to a short list (Shannon mentioned ~4), then Shannon and at least one other person conduct the interview.
6. **Offer:** If selected, the offer is extended through the staffing company, not directly by LADBS — the staffing company then becomes Eric's employer of record for the contract.

## Rate

- **Rate cap:** $165/hour — described as the maximum LADBS can pay for this position. Shannon shared this cap in confidence and asked Eric not to reveal to CPS that he knows the exact cap number.
- **The contracting company takes a cut** of the billed rate before paying the contractor, so the $165 is not what Eric would personally take home.
- **Negotiate the rate with CPS**, not with LADBS directly — LADBS isn't part of that negotiation.
- **Reference point Shannon gave:** contractors he typically works with (mostly .NET developers, a somewhat lower-skill tier than a systems architect role) land around $100–120/hour, implying a systems architect position should reasonably command more.
- **Cost is scored:** Rate makes up **10%** of the overall proposal evaluation. Coming in too high risks being cut first, so Shannon advised finding a "happy medium" rather than maximizing the number.
- **Factor in the lack of benefits** when setting a target rate — no employer-paid health insurance (COBRA cost falls on the contractor) and generally no paid vacation as a 1099/contract hire, so the hourly number needs to cover what a base salary + benefits package would have included.
