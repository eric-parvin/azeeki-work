# Qualification

Screening out bad-fit opportunities is worth more than any objection-handling script.
Most deals lost on rate were deals that should never have been entered.

## Qualify In

- A named executive sponsor exists and can be reached
- Budget authority is identified, even if the number is not yet approved
- There is a deadline, mandate, or licensing event creating urgency
- The problem is described in terms of risk, adoption, or business outcome
- Failure has a consequence the customer can articulate

## Qualify Out

- No executive sponsor and no path to one
- Nobody in the conversation controls budget
- The ask is "we need someone to build reports" or "we need a DAX resource"
- The stated ceiling is below the floor rate and will not move
- The customer is collecting bids to benchmark a staffing agency
- The customer will not commit to a discovery conversation before pricing

## Disqualification Language

> Based on what you've described, I don't think I'm the right fit, and I'd rather tell
> you that now than take your money. What you need is an implementation resource. If it
> would help, I'm glad to write up what that role should look like so you can hire well.

This costs nothing, protects the rate, and frequently causes the customer to re-scope
the engagement upward.

# Customer Discovery Process

## Step 1: Understand the Business Driver

Ask:

- Why is this project being undertaken?
- What business outcome is expected?
- Is there an executive mandate?
- Is there a licensing deadline?
- Is there a modernization initiative?
- What happens if the project fails?

Goal:

Move the conversation from technology to business value.

---

## Step 2: Understand the Risk

Ask:

- How many users are impacted?
- Which reports are mission critical?
- Are executives using these reports?
- How complex are the calculations?
- How much business logic exists today?
- What timeline constraints exist?

Goal:

Help the customer recognize business risk.

---

## Step 3: Define Success

Ask:

"What does success look like two years after the migration is complete?"

Possible answers:

- Tableau fully retired
- Reduced licensing costs
- Standardized governance
- Trusted reporting platform
- Faster delivery model
- Fabric adoption

Goal:

Define measurable outcomes.

## Quote One Number, Never a Range

Quoting "$225 to $275" concedes $50 an hour before the customer speaks. A range is heard
as a ceiling, and the customer will only ever hear the bottom of it.

Quote a single number. If flexibility needs to be signaled, signal it with **scope**, not
with the rate.

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "I'm in the $225 to $275 range." | "My rate is $275 an hour." |
| "It depends, somewhere around $250." | "$275. Let's talk about how much of my time you actually need." |
| "I could probably do $225." | "There's flexibility on scope and commitment. What can you commit to?" |

Silence after the number is the tool. The first person to speak after a price concedes.

# Handling Rate Objections

Answer every objection with a question. Never answer an objection with a number.

Customer:

> Your rate is higher than expected.

Response:

> I understand. Can you help me understand what range you were expecting and what role you're looking for?

Listen first. The answer reveals whether this is a budget problem or a role-definition
problem. Those require completely different responses, and guessing wrong loses the deal.

---

## If Customer Says $150/hr

Response:

> That's a real number for implementation work: report builders, DAX resources, offshore
> development. Those are legitimate roles and you will need them.
>
> What I do is different. I make sure the architecture those people build against is
> correct, so you aren't paying to redo it in eighteen months.
>
> If $150 is the budget, the honest answer is that you should hire an implementation
> resource, and I'd be glad to help you scope that role.

The closing offer to walk away is the most effective sentence in this document.
Willingness to lose the deal is the only real leverage available, and it converts more
often than discounting does.

---

## If Customer Asks "Can You Do Better?"

Response:

> There's flexibility depending on scope, duration, and commitment. If you can commit to
> a hundred hours or a multi-month engagement, I can work with you on the rate. What kind
> of commitment are you in a position to make?

Never answer the discount question with a discount. Answer it with a question about
commitment.

## The Scope Reduction Play

The highest-value alternative to discounting. Use when the rate is genuinely immovable
but the opportunity is real.

> I can't move the rate, but I can move the scope. Let's start with the assessment. It's
> a fixed fee, it's bounded, and at the end you'll have a roadmap you own regardless of
> whether we continue. That's a much smaller decision for you to make right now.

This converts a rate objection into a small yes. Small yes leads to large yes. A
discounted large yes leads to a discounted renewal, permanently.

# Discount Ladder

Maximum discount is 10%, under any circumstances, for any customer.

| Customer commits to | Discount | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing | 0% | $275 |
| 100+ hour block, prepaid | 5% | $261 |
| 3-month retainer, prepaid | 7% | $256 |
| 6-month retainer, prepaid | 10% | $248 |

The 6-month tier is the only permitted rate below floor, and only because prepayment
eliminates collection risk and removes further sales cost.

Rules:

- The discount is applied **after** the commitment is in writing, never before
- Verbal intent is not commitment
- Discounts do not stack with fixed-fee packages
- A discount granted once becomes the renewal anchor forever; treat it as permanent

# Practice Economics

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Target personal income | $250,000 |
| Business costs | $25,000 |
| Insurance | $10,000 |
| Marketing | $5,000 |
| Reserve | $10,000 |
| **Target revenue** | **$300,000** |

Billable hours are the variable that determines the floor. A solo practice also performs
its own selling, marketing, contracting, and delivery administration, so realistic
utilization is 850 to 1,000 billable hours per year — not 1,200.

| Billable hours | Required rate | Realistic? |
|---|---|---|
| 1,200 | $250 | No. Assumes 25 billable hrs/week, 48 weeks, 100% conversion. |
| 1,000 | $300 | Stretch target for a strong year. |
| 950 | $316 | Realistic planning assumption. |
| 850 | $353 | Ramp year or heavy sales cycle. |

The $250 floor is therefore a **hard stop, not a target**. Sustained work at $250 does
not reach the revenue goal at achievable utilization. Every hour billed at floor must be
offset by hours billed at the Architecture or Executive tier.

Review actual utilization quarterly and reset the floor if it drifts below 850 hours
annualized.

# Call Execution Plan

## Before the Call

- Confirm the opportunity passes qualification
- Decide the tier and the fixed-fee package in advance
- Write the floor down and keep it visible: **$250**
- Confirm who on the call holds budget authority

## During the Call — Fixed Sequence

1. **Business driver.** Why now, what is the mandate, what is the deadline.
2. **Risk.** How many users, which reports are mission critical, are executives on them,
   what happens if this fails.
3. **Success definition.** "What does success look like two years after this is done?"
4. **Reframe.** Move from report conversion to transformation language.
5. **State the rate.** Once. Single number. Then stop talking.
6. **Objections.** Answer with questions, never with numbers.
7. **Close to a next step**, not a signature: "Let me put together a one-page scope for
   the assessment and send it Thursday."

Never skip to step 5. Pricing before discovery forfeits the reframe, and without the
reframe the rate has nothing to stand on.

## After the Call — Within 24 Hours

- Send a written recap that leads with **their** stated risks and success criteria
- Put the rate at the bottom, after the value is restated in their own words
- Include the fixed-fee assessment as the recommended entry point
- Name a specific date for the next step

## Ongoing

- Every engagement ends with a proposal for the next one
- Every assessment names the follow-on architecture work in its final section
- Raise the rate $25 every twelve months for new clients
- Existing clients receive 60 days notice at renewal
- Review utilization quarterly and reset the floor if it drifts
