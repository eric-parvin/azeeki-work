## Questions to Ask When Talking With CPAs

### Business structure
- Since the LLC is jointly owned with my spouse and North Carolina isn't a community-property state, does this default to partnership taxation (Form 1065 + K-1s to each of us), or are there other options?
- At what revenue level would an S-corp election make sense, and what would "reasonable compensation" look like if we did that?
- Does the planned rename (Javia Company LLC → Azeeki LLC on 1/1/2027) require anything special for this year's filing, or is it clean since it's effective the following January?

### Combining multiple income types
- Between two W-2 employers this year (Microsoft, then CPS) plus 1099 income from the LLC, is my withholding likely to cover my total liability, or do I need to make estimated payments for the remainder of 2026?
- How do I estimate what to set aside from the 1099 contract for federal/state income tax and self-employment tax?
- The UNC contract runs into 2027 — how should that income be recognized/reported across the two tax years?

### Severance
- How is the lump-sum severance taxed, and is the standard supplemental-wage withholding likely to be enough given my total income this year?
- Does receiving severance affect eligibility for unemployment benefits, and does that interact with anything tax-wise?

### RSU sale
- How do I confirm the correct cost basis (ordinary income already taxed at vesting vs. gain/loss on sale)?
- Was the sale short-term or long-term, and is there anything worth doing before year-end to manage the gain?

### Self-employment / 1099 specifics
- What business expenses can the LLC deduct (home office, equipment, software, mileage, etc.)?
- Should I set up a SEP IRA or Solo 401(k) this year to reduce taxable income?
- Can I deduct health insurance premiums (COBRA or marketplace) against the 1099 income?

### Working with you
- What's your fee structure — hourly, flat rate for a return like mine, or an ongoing advisory retainer?
- Given how much is happening this year, would you recommend filing an extension rather than rushing by April?
- What documents should I bring to our first real meeting?
- How often would we check in — just at tax time, or quarterly given the moving pieces?

### Documents to gather before the call
- Final Microsoft pay stub / W-2 (once issued)
- Severance agreement and any payout confirmation
- RSU vesting and sale statements (from your brokerage, e.g., Fidelity/Schwab equity plan account)
- CPS contract/offer details and pay stubs once issued
- UNC-Chapel Hill SOW / contract and any invoices sent so far
- LLC formation documents and EIN letter
- Business and personal bank statements (BoA business account, personal accounts)

## What the CPA Will Likely Ask You

A first call or intake conversation is usually them scoping your situation before quoting a fee. Good to have answers ready for:

- **Filing basics** — filing status (married filing jointly, with your spouse), state of residence (NC), any dependents.
- **What you need** — just this year's tax prep, or ongoing advisory/quarterly check-ins/bookkeeping too? (Worth deciding your answer before the call, since it affects their quote.)
- **All income sources** — they'll want the same rundown that's in the summary above: two W-2 employers this year (Microsoft, then CPS), 1099 income through the LLC, the RSU sale, and the severance. Be ready to give rough total income and timing for each.
- **Business entity details** — that the LLC (Javia Company LLC, becoming Azeeki LLC) is jointly owned with your spouse, whether it's currently taxed as a sole proprietorship/disregarded entity or has ever filed a partnership return, and whether it has an EIN and business bank account already (it does).
- **Estimated tax payments made so far** — whether you've sent any quarterly estimated payments in 2026, and if not, how much withholding has already happened via your W-2 jobs.
- **Prior-year return** — whether they can see last year's filed return, mainly to check for carryovers and confirm nothing unusual.
- **Bookkeeping status** — whether you're tracking business income/expenses anywhere yet (spreadsheet, QuickBooks, or nothing formal so far).
- **Timeline** — how soon you need things sorted (e.g., before Q4 estimated payments are due, or before year-end for any planning moves like retirement contributions).
- **Budget/fee comfort** — some will ask directly what you're expecting to pay, to make sure their pricing tier is a fit before going further.

Having rough numbers ready for total income by source (even estimates) will make the first call much more productive than trying to reconstruct it live.
